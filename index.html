Where the money comes from

One channel carries roughly half of all revenue.

Email produced 62.1% of March revenue and 47.1% of April's. The March peak and the April decline are largely the same story: campaign volume rose, then returned to normal. Channel-level revenue is available from February onward.

    The funnel

    Product pages improved every month. The cart broke in April.

    Percentages are the share of users at each stage who reached the next one, counted as unique users. User-level funnel data starts in February. Product view to cart rose in every month measured. Cart to checkout slipped in March and fell sharply in April, which is where the month lost its orders.

    April funnel

    Unique users at each stage, April 1-30, with the share advancing to the next and the March rate for comparison

    If the share of cart users who started checkout had held at March's 73.15% and the later stages performed exactly as they did in April, about that many more users would have purchased. The dollar value applies April's average order. This is the single largest recoverable gap in the data.

    Pages

    Traffic moved back to category browsing.

    Change in page views, April against March, for April's ten most-viewed pages. Collection pages grew while the flagship product page fell, the reverse of March, when email campaigns sent visitors straight to product pages.

    Engagement quality

    Fewer visitors, each worth more attention.

    Every engagement measure improved in April against March, even as sessions fell 14.50%. The sessions that disappeared were largely short campaign visits; what remained engaged more deeply.

    Show the underlying data tables

    Windows. January 1-30, February 1-28, March 1-30 and April 1-30, 2026. Volume metrics are shown as daily averages wherever the months are compared side by side; rates and ratios are independent of window length and are shown directly. January figures come from the February-March report and cover January 1-30; channel-level revenue is not available for January.

    Definitions. Orders are completed purchase events. The funnel is measured on unique users, because the same visitor can fire an event several times (add_to_cart about 2.6 times per user in April). Unique users per stage are not strictly nested, so the rates are close approximations. Event-count rates appear in the data tables as a cross-check; the View to order tile uses event counts because user-level data is not available for January. Revenue is recorded against the purchase event and is not attributable to individual pages. The scroll event is excluded throughout: GA4 records it only at 90% page depth and it fires on roughly 5% of page views, so its measurement is being verified before use.
